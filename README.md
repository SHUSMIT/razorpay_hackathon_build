# Fraud Risk Manager

**Razorpay AI Buildathon — Track 02, AI Risk Manager**

A merchant-facing transaction risk service. It scores a payment, explains the
score **in language a human reviewer can act on**, makes a **bounded** decision
(`allow` / `review` / `block`), refuses to block more of the merchant's traffic
than a hard cap allows, routes borderline cases to a real human queue, and
writes every decision to an append-only audit log.

Strictly defense-only. Nothing here helps anyone evade fraud detection.

---

## The problem

Fraud and chargebacks eat merchant margin, but the obvious fix eats more of it.
A model tuned to catch every fraud declines real customers at checkout, and
legitimate transactions outnumber fraud roughly **570 to 1** in this data — so
a model that is slightly too aggressive costs the merchant more in lost good
customers than the fraud ever did.

The interesting question is therefore not "can you detect fraud" but **"where
do you put the threshold, what stops the system when it goes wrong, and can a
human understand why it decided what it decided."** This project answers all
three, in money.

## What the data actually said

Four measured findings changed this model, and each one would have inflated the
headline number if left unchecked. Full evidence in **[docs/FINDINGS.md](docs/FINDINGS.md)**.

1. **A random split scores 685x higher, and it is a lie.** With only 2,000
   cardholders, income/debt/credit-score columns form a near-unique fingerprint
   of a *person*. A random split lets the model recognise who was defrauded
   before; a time-based split does not. Removing identity and calendar-drifting
   features took validation PR-AUC from 0.089% to 4.657% — a 52x improvement.

2. **`merchant_state` was a lookup table.** Alone it scored 89.4% PR-AUC —
   better than the entire model — because 95–98% of later-period fraud sits in
   a single value. It is a label artefact, not a signal, and it is dropped.

3. **The fraud mechanism inverts halfway through the timeline.** Fraud is 83%
   card-not-present in the training era and 86–89% chip-present in the recent
   era. Eight years of history was teaching the wrong pattern. Training weights
   now decay exponentially with age, with the half-life **tuned by Optuna**
   rather than asserted: 15.2% → 46.0% on validation.

4. **A 5% subsample ranks hyper-parameters by noise.** It leaves ~508 frauds.
   The search set keeps *every* fraud and subsamples only negatives.

## Explaining a decision to a human

The predecessor of this pipeline was trained on anonymised PCA components whose
meanings were never published. A reviewer could be told a score, never a reason.
This dataset has real named columns, so the same machinery now produces
something a person can act on — and **every number is measured on the training
split, never invented**:

```
Decision: BLOCK      risk 91.4%

  ▲  merchant category: Money Transfer — 2.1% of transactions in this
     group were fraud, against 0.15% overall (14x)
  ▲  transaction amount: 1,204.00 — higher than 99.6% of legitimate
     transactions
  ▲  how the card was presented: Chip Transaction
  ▼  the terminal reported an error: no

  Historically, transactions scoring in this band were fraud 87% of the
  time (312 of 359 comparable transactions).
```

For anonymised columns this degrades honestly to *"more extreme than 99.7% of
legitimate transactions"* rather than a fabricated business meaning. **We never
invent a semantic for a column whose meaning was not published.**

## Bounded decisioning

Two limits sit between the model and the merchant's customers:

- **A review band sized by capacity, not by a magic constant.** A review team
  can look at a fixed share of traffic per day; that operational limit decides
  the band width. Under the previous rule-of-thumb the band caught 0.016% of
  traffic and the human queue was decorative.
- **A hard cap on the automatic block rate.** The service will never auto-block
  more than 5% of a rolling 200-decision window. Past that, blocks are
  force-downgraded to `review` with the reason logged. Drift, an attack, or a
  bad deploy degrades this into a review queue rather than a merchant outage.
  The cap is enforced from the very first request against a minimum-sample
  floor, so a cold start cannot slip a burst through.

## Running it

```bash
python run.py setup     # pinned dependencies
python run.py all       # data → tune → train → assess → context → test
python run.py api       # terminal 1: risk service on 127.0.0.1:8000
python run.py app       # terminal 2: dashboard on localhost:8501
```

`run.py all` is resumable. Optuna studies are SQLite-backed and each model is
checkpointed the moment it is fitted, so an interrupted run picks up where it
stopped rather than starting over.

**Data.** Place the Kaggle archive contents in `data/raw/ibm/`
(`transactions_data.csv`, `cards_data.csv`, `users_data.csv`, `mcc_codes.json`,
`train_fraud_labels.json`). The raw and processed data are gitignored; model
artefacts and reports are committed on purpose, because they are the evidence a
reviewer reads.

**Machine budget.** Training is capped to half the logical cores so the machine
stays usable, and CatBoost's categorical-combination depth is capped, which is
the setting that actually controls its memory appetite. `used_ram_limit` is a
hint to CatBoost's allocator rather than an enforced cap — the real safety net
is that a failed fit retries on fewer *negative* rows, keeping every fraud, and
says so in the report. Override with `FRM_THREADS` and `FRM_RAM_GB`.

## Known limitations

- **This dataset is at least partly synthetic.** Its patterns are cleaner than
  real payment traffic. Findings 2 and 3 above are artefacts of the generator,
  not facts about the world. The *method* transfers; these numbers do not.
- **Selection optimism remains.** The model and the recency half-life are both
  chosen on validation, so validation numbers flatter. The test figure is the
  one to quote.
- **An aggressive half-life shrinks the effective sample.** Most of the 7.3M
  training rows contribute little once decay is applied; the effective sample
  size is reported alongside the score.
- **No drift monitoring yet.** The pipeline *detects* drift between periods but
  does not watch for it in production. That is the next thing to build, and it
  matters more here than anywhere given finding 3.
- **No real payment-gateway integration.** The service takes a transaction over
  HTTP; it is not wired to a live processor.
- **The review queue is single-tenant and in-process.** Fine for a demo,
  not for production, where it would be a durable queue with per-reviewer
  authentication.
- **LightGBM is absent** — its native library is blocked by an Application
  Control policy on the build machine, so the ensemble is three families rather
  than four.
